The CareNest app sends login codes and visit messages by SMS. In India, a business SMS gets delivered only if the business has registered itself and the exact wording of the message. That registration has to be done by your business, with your PAN and GST. Here is the one-page version. Flip the switch at the top for the full guide.

The one thing to remember

Nobody can log in to the live app until the login-code SMS is approved. Login is phone number plus a one-time code, and there is no other way in. This approval is the longest wait in the whole project, so the clock should start now.

What is DLT?

A government rule (from TRAI, the telecom regulator) that every business SMS must come from a registered business, under a registered sender name, using wording approved in advance.

Jio, Airtel, Vi and BSNL check every message against that register and block anything that doesn't match.

What is MSG91?

The SMS company our app already uses. Our server hands each message to MSG91, and MSG91 passes it on to the phone networks.

MSG91 cannot register on your behalf. It only carries messages that you have registered.

1What this is and why it matters

CareNest talks to people by SMS. Users get their login code and booking updates. Nurses have no app, so SMS and email are the only way they hear about a visit. In India, every business SMS has to pass a check run by the phone companies under TRAI's rules (the TCCCPR regulations of 2018). The check is called DLT. Your business must register itself, its sender name and the exact wording of each message. Because the register is tied to the business's PAN and GST, we can't do it for you. We can prepare everything, though, so your part is mostly signing up, uploading documents and clicking approve.

Why this is urgent

Production login cannot go live until the login-code template is DLT-approved. The app has no password login, only phone number plus a one-time code. Without an approved template the phone networks drop the code, and nobody can sign in. This is the longest wait in the project, which is why we're asking you to start this week.

2DLT explained: who is who

DLT stands for Distributed Ledger Technology. It's a shared register kept by all the phone companies. Register on one company's portal and the details reach the others.8 Four parties are involved:

Principal Entity (PE)

Your business. The sender in the eyes of the law. Owns the registration, the sender name and the templates.

Telemarketer (TM)

MSG91. The company that delivers messages for you. Since Dec 2024 your registration must name it, or messages are rejected.2,7

DLT portal

The phone company's website where you register: Jio TrueConnect, Airtel, Vi VILPower, SmartPing, BSNL, Tata and others.8

Header (sender ID)

The name people see as the sender, for example CRNEST. Since May 2025 the phone shows a letter after it for the message type: -S service, -T transactional, -P promotional.18

If any part doesn't match, the network drops the message. That covers a word that differs from the template, a link that isn't registered, or MSG91 missing from your registration.1,6

Rules that changed recently

Many online guides are out of date. These are the rules our templates follow:

  • Links and phone numbers must be registered (since Oct 2024). Any web link, app link or callback number in an SMS has to be on your approved list ("CTA whitelisting"). If it isn't, the message is blocked.19,6
  • MSG91 must be linked to your registration (since Dec 2024). This is the "PE-TM chain". Without it, every message is rejected, OTPs included.2,7
  • Stricter rules (Feb 2025 amendment). A message that mixes service and promotional content counts as promotional. If a template is misused, operators can suspend all of the sender's traffic immediately.3
  • Typed blanks (TRAI direction of 18 Nov 2025). Each blank in a template must say what goes in it: a number, text, a link, a phone number or an email. The old generic blank {#var#} is no longer accepted for new templates.1,4,22

4Our 7 messages as DLT-ready templates

Each template is fixed text plus a few typed blanks. The blanks follow the format in Vi's current manual.4 Some portals display the same tags with longer names, such as {#alphanumeric#}.22

The blanks
  • {#num#} digits only, up to 40
  • {#alp#} letters and numbers, up to 40, must not start with http or www
  • {#urg#} a registered web link, up to 120
  • {#cbn#} a registered phone number (not used by us)4
Rules we designed around
  • At most 3 blanks per template. More needs a written justification.4
  • A new template is linked to one sender name.4
  • The brand name sits in the fixed text, and a blank is never the last word.9
  • Only text blanks may sit side by side.4
  • "#", "Rs" and commas live in the fixed text. Amounts go as 1600, not 1,600.22
Which category

Messages 2 to 7: Service, called "Service Implicit" on portals that still use the older names. They're triggered by a booking someone already made, so they reach numbers on Do Not Disturb, at any hour, and show -S.22,23

Message 1, the login code: depends on the portal. Vi's current manual puts app-login OTPs from non-bank businesses under Transactional (-T).4 MSG91 and several 2026 guides still say non-banks must use Service Implicit.9,22 We'll pick whichever the chosen portal lists for app-login OTPs. Never Promotional or Service Explicit, because those don't reach Do Not Disturb numbers.

2. New visit

To nurseService

Decided: Option A below. The draft message has about ten changing values: visit number, date, time, hours, care type, patient name, age, sex, phone, address and a map link. The new rules allow 3 blanks, each up to 40 characters. The sample address alone ("H. No. 221, Sector 14, Bareilly, near City Park gate") is 52 characters. Two things can't go in at all:

  • The patient's phone number. Phone numbers in an SMS have to be registered in advance, and a patient's number changes with every booking. Hiding it inside a text blank is exactly what the Nov 2025 rule was made to stop. Operators can suspend all of a sender's SMS for template misuse,1,3 which would take login codes down too.
  • A Google Maps link, unless it's registered. In principle it can be registered as a dynamic link (everything after the ? changes). We couldn't confirm that portals accept a link on a domain you don't own, such as google.com.

Medical condition notes stay out of SMS in both options, as already decided.

Keep messages plain

We use plain English letters only: "Rs" instead of ₹, straight quotes, no emoji. Any special character turns the SMS into a Unicode SMS, which fits far fewer characters, so a message can split into two billed SMS. Every template above stays within one SMS. No template contains a promotion, because a single "book again and save" line would turn the whole message into an advert.3

5MSG91 setup and how our app uses it

MSG91 is a messaging company based in Indore, run by Walkover Web Solutions.14 Our app talks to it in two ways:

Login codes: MSG91's OTP service

Already built in version 1. Our server asks MSG91 to send a code to a number. MSG91 creates and checks the code, using a template linked to the approved login-code template.

Everything else: MSG91 template SMS

New in version 2. Messages 2 to 7 each have their own MSG91 template, linked to its own DLT Template ID. Our server sends the template name and the values for the blanks.

Every message our server sends is saved. If MSG91 or the network fails, the server tries again. In the admin panel each message shows whether it was delivered, with a Resend button.

In MSG91 you'll see three IDs: the Entity ID (your business), attached to the sender name CRNEST, and one DLT Template ID per message.12,13 They are not secret. The API key is, so treat it like a bank password.

Help from MSG91. MSG91 has a DLT help desk and step-by-step guides for each portal. It can also nudge SmartPing about slow applications if you share the application number.8 It cannot register for you, because the registration has to be in your business's name.9

For the developers
  • v1 OTP: https://control.msg91.com/api/v5/otp with MSG91_AUTH_KEY and MSG91_OTP_TEMPLATE_ID (the MSG91 template ID, which carries the DLT template ID). With no auth key the backend logs the code (dev mode).
  • v2: MSG91 template/Flow SMS API, one MSG91 template per message, each mapped to its DLT Template ID and to CRNEST. MSG91 templates use ##name## placeholders, while DLT uses typed tags. The rendered text must match the DLT template character for character, and each value must be 40 characters or less, or MSG91 returns "SMS not matched with DLT template".9,13
  • Before sending: strip commas from amounts and collapse double spaces. Truncate {#alp#} values to 40 characters and keep them from starting with http/www. Keep the link at 120 characters or less.
  • Not verified: whether punctuation (comma, colon, full stop) inside an {#alp#} value passes scrubbing. Vi's manual only says "letters and numbers".4 Test sample values like "Sat 10 Oct, 3:00 PM" in the portal preview, and fall back to "Sat 10 Oct 3 PM" if needed.
  • Option A link: /v?k=<random token>, query-string only (no dynamic path), no redirect. The page should expire after the visit and be marked noindex.5,19

7What happens until approval

Keeps working
  • All development and testing carry on.
  • On our test server, login codes show up in the server log, so testers can sign in without SMS.
  • Nurse and admin alerts go by email.
  • Test SMS go only to a short list of our own test numbers.
